Osaka Expo 2025

OverviewIn depthThemesQuestionsAnnotated bibliographyFurther readingOn the web

Osaka Expo 2025 was an international exposition planned for Osaka, Japan, in 2025, under the theme "Designing future society for our lives" (Peterson 2020p. 19). Peterson writes that Japan's bid succeeded against competition from Russia and Azerbaijan (Peterson 2020p. 281). The texts do not name the organisers. Both the introduction and the conclusion treat it as a future event, with an anticipated attendance of 28 million (Peterson 2020p. 19p. 290). The author sees it as evidence that Japan remained a host of expositions and was seeking economic revival through one (Peterson 2020p. 289p. 290).

In depth: what our sources say

Written from the texts listed in the annotated bibliography below and from nothing else. Each reference in brackets leads to the text the statement rests on.

Announcement and theme

Peterson's introduction places Osaka 2025 after Milan and Dubai 2020, and describes both as future-oriented in theme (Peterson 2020p. 19). The author adds that non-state actors, such as corporations, take an increasingly important role at these fairs (Peterson 2020p. 19). A footnote gives the theme of the 2025 expo as "Designing future society for our lives" and cites a 2018 article in the Japan Times (Peterson 2020p. 19).

Bid and organisers

The texts do not say who organised the exposition. The conclusion states that Japan was successful in its bid to host the 2025 international expo, and that its competitors came from Russia and Azerbaijan rather than from Europe or the United States (Peterson 2020p. 281). The author dates the announcement that Osaka would host the 2025 expo to late 2018 (Peterson 2020p. 289).

Attendance and expectations

For 2025 the conclusion gives an "anticipated attendance of 28 million", which it calls not significantly above the attendance of the Aichi and Tsukuba fairs (Peterson 2020p. 290). It sets this beside the Osaka expo that Japan had already hosted, with its "massive attendance figure of 66.2 million", a figure attributed to the BIE and said to have exceeded that of the 1964-1965 New York fair (Peterson 2020p. 289). That sentence gives no year, but it stands in a list of fairs Japan has held, and the same page names Expo 1970 in Osaka (Peterson 2020p. 289).

Economic purpose

Peterson reads the fair mainly as an economic project. It appears that Japan hoped to revive the faltering economy of the Osaka region in 2025, drawing on the momentum of the 2020 Tokyo Olympics (Peterson 2020p. 290). The author argues that the exercise is largely driven by an agenda of economic revival, which spreads large Olympic-related infrastructure spending beyond the Tokyo area (Peterson 2020p. 290).

Its place in the series

Peterson places the fair in a sequence of Japanese and Asian expositions. Expo 1970 in Osaka presented Japan as having embraced the future, and the 2025 announcement comes some fifty-five years after it (Peterson 2020p. 289). The author dates the shift of the host role to Asia to Osaka in 1970 and Shanghai in 2010 (Peterson 2020p. 280). Japan also held smaller specialised fairs, including Expo 2005 in Aichi and fairs at Tsukuba (1985) and Okinawa (1975) (Peterson 2020p. 290). Shanghai, which the text calls the most-attended international exposition in Asia, drew 73.1 million visitors and is the comparison for the Osaka forecast (Peterson 2020p. 290). The conclusion suggests that "Yet it seems more likely 2025 will be the last" major international exposition in Japan, since its anticipated attendance is only modestly above Milan's 21.5 million (Peterson 2020p. 290).

Themes in the scholarship

  • Future-oriented expositions — the introduction groups Osaka 2025 with Dubai 2020 as future-oriented in theme, and quotes its planned theme in a footnote (Peterson 2020p. 19)
  • Economic revival — the conclusion reads the fair as an effort to revive the Osaka economy and to spread Olympic-era spending beyond Tokyo (Peterson 2020p. 290)
  • Attendance comparisons — the text sets the anticipated 28 million against the 66.2 million of the earlier Osaka expo, the 1964-1965 New York fair, Shanghai's 73.1 million and Milan's 21.5 million (Peterson 2020p. 289)
  • Japan's exposition series — Expo 1970 and the smaller Japanese fairs form the context for 2025 (Peterson 2020p. 289p. 290)
  • Asia as host — the author argues that Asia has taken over the host role from the West (Peterson 2020p. 280)

Questions for study

  1. The conclusion sets an anticipated 28 million visitors for 2025 against 66.2 million at the earlier Osaka expo (Peterson 2020p. 289p. 290). What does the gap suggest about the changed place of expositions in Japan?
  2. Peterson treats Osaka 2025 as a way to revive a regional economy, using momentum from the Tokyo Olympics (Peterson 2020p. 290). What evidence would show whether a fair achieves economic revival?
  3. The conclusion suggests that 2025 may be the last major international exposition in Japan (Peterson 2020p. 290). What would make an exposition major in this argument?

Moderate discussion 1

Peterson, William. Asian Self-Representation at World’s Fairs. Amsterdam: Amsterdam University Press, 2020.
Introduction (ch. 1), pp. 19-20; Conclusion (ch. 10), pp. 281, 289-290

Peterson's study of how Asian nations represented themselves at Western world's fairs between 1915 and 2015 mentions Osaka 2025 in its introduction and conclusion. The introduction places it after Milan, alongside Dubai 2020, as a future-oriented expo in which corporations play a growing part, and it gives the planned theme. The conclusion instead says Japan has already hosted it, with a reported attendance of 66.2 million, and argues that the fair was driven by economic revival and was probably Japan's last major international exposition. The larger case is that state-sponsored representation by Asian nations at expositions in the West has diminished, and that Asia has taken over from the West as the main host.

About the work as a whole Asia: central

Peterson's book examines how independent Asian states sought to control their own representation at Western world's fairs between 1915 and 2015. Its case studies are San Francisco 1915, New York 1939-40, Brisbane 1988, New York 1964-65 and Milan 2015, each centred on the pavilions or concessions of Japan, China, Thailand, the Philippines, Indonesia or South Korea. The introduction argues that Japan, and to a lesser extent Thailand, were arguably the only Asian nations that consistently presented themselves, and proposes a move from a colonial order of display to a performative order of live encounters, drawing on Mitchell and Greenhalgh. Each case is a chapter, organised by fair, using archival and ethnographic methods. The conclusion argues that state-directed national representation has declined as the form became costly for host cities, and that Asia has become the leading host of expositions.

East and Southeast Asia in this work. East and Southeast Asia are the whole subject of this work, and their coverage is central. Japan is the main case in chapters 2 and 4 (the government site, 'Japan Beautiful' concession and fine arts galleries at San Francisco 1915; the pavilion and Takarazuka Revue at New York 1939-40). China fills chapter 3 (the official site and the Chinese Village at San Francisco 1915, with Chinatown's role) and chapter 5 (Expo '88 in Brisbane). The Philippines (chapter 6) and Indonesia (chapter 7) are studied through their New York 1964-65 pavilions, South Korea (chapter 8) and Thailand (chapter 9) through Milan 2015, and chapter 10 treats the Chinese Milan pavilion and Asia's rise as host. Most of the participation is by states, commissioners and merchants: the Japanese Commission, the Chinese commissioners Chen Qi and T.C. Chu, and the Six Companies running the Joy Zone village. Displays organised by others are also discussed. Chapter 2 notes that a Formosa tea house names a Japanese colony and reads Japan's Red Cross display as propaganda, and chapter 4 describes New York critics who wanted Japan as cherry blossoms and willowy maidens. Asian women are mainly performers and press subjects, notably in chapters 6 and 7. Asian visitors feature only as crowds at the Chinese Village groundbreaking. Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos, Burma and the Malay states are not discussed in the chapter overviews.

On the web

Wikipedia

Expo 2025 (2025年日本国際博覧会, Nisennijūgo-nen Nippon Kokusai Hakurankai; and officially 大阪・関西万博, Ōsaka–Kansai Banpaku for short) was a World Expo organized and sanctioned by the Bureau International des Expositions (BIE), which was held in Osaka, Japan. It was open for six months, from 13 April to 13 October 2025. This was the second time that Osaka Prefecture has hosted a World Expo, having previously hosted Expo 1970 in Suita. Expo 2025 marked the return to the traditional 5-year scheduling cycle after the 2020 edition in Dubai was delayed to 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The total visitor count was 29,017,924 (paid attendance 25,578,986).
